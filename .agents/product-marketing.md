# Product Marketing Context — COMOOTD

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-09-10
**Status:** Working context drafted from the owner's instructions and existing product; unconfirmed business details remain unknown.

## Product Overview
COMOOTD helps people discover curated outfits, inspect their products, and follow links to external stores. Curators publish looks containing 2–5 products; Journal adds styling guidance. It is a fashion discovery and curation platform, not an on-site checkout.
Business model: external affiliate/product links are supported. Pricing, commissions and service promises are not established in this brief.

## Target Audience
Owner-confirmed: Indonesian Gen Z, comfortable with natural English–Indonesian fashion language. Two journeys: visitors seeking outfit inspiration and curators sharing their taste. Do not assume every visitor is a fashion expert.
Jobs: find a look for daily plans; identify and save its pieces; share curated looks with product links.

## Problems & Pain Points
Working hypothesis, not customer research: outfit indecision and inspiration disconnected from product details. Avoid shame, body judgments and pressure to buy an entire look.

## Competitive Landscape
Not researched. Alternative behaviors may include social inspiration feeds and marketplace search. Do not claim superiority over named competitors.

## Differentiation
Visible product capabilities: whole looks with product details, creator attribution, saved collections, external shop links, styling articles, curator publishing workflow. No quantified outcome claims.

## Objections
Where do I buy? At the linked store, not COMOOTD checkout.
Must I sign in to browse? Public catalogue is accessible without an account.
Are drafts synced? No: device/browser-local drafts, vulnerable to browser data deletion.
Anti-persona: users expecting guaranteed inventory, real-time prices or native checkout.

## Switching Dynamics
Hypotheses: frustration with disconnected outfit/product discovery; attraction to clear product references; familiar social scrolling; uncertainty about links and fit. Not validated through interviews.

## Customer Language
No consumer interviews or testimonials supplied. Owner's explicit direction: target Gen Z and use mixed English, not formal full-Indonesian copy.
Use: look, outfit, style, vibe, mix & match, weekend plans, upload, link; conversational kamu/kami.
Avoid: forced slang, viral catchphrases, corporate jargon, repetitive kurasi/terkurator, unverified 'terbukti', guaranteed personalization or earnings.
Glossary: look = curated outfit; produk = individual item; koleksi = saved grouping; kurator = creator; draf = unpublished device-local work; terbitkan = public publication. Likes and saves are different signals.

## Brand Voice
Fashion-savvy, conversational, confident, inclusive, effortless. English can carry editorial expression; Indonesian adds practical context. Never force a language ratio. Keep OUTFIT CHECK, DONE RIGHT., Match Your Vibe and Tailored for You. Instructions remain short and explicit. Errors are calm and actionable.

## Proof Points
No verified performance metrics, testimonials or audience counts. Dummy content must not be turned into social proof. Explain implemented capabilities instead. Do not invent response times, free-service promises or affiliation guarantees.

## Goals
Primary visitor action: explore a look, then inspect products and optionally open a store. Secondary: save and follow. Curator action: prepare and publish a look after review. Traffic acquisition mix and conversion baselines unknown.
